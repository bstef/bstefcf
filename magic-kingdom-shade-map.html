How this is calculated (and its limits)

Sun elevation, azimuth, sunrise, sunset and solar noon are computed with a standard NOAA solar-position formula for the Magic Kingdom's coordinates (28.4160°N, 81.5812°W), converted to Orlando's local clock (handles EST/EDT automatically). That part is precise astronomy.

The attraction shade ratings are a different kind of estimate. There's no public 3D model of every building, awning and tree canopy in the park, so instead each attraction gets a baseline coverage type from how its queue is generally built:

  • Fully covered — indoor or fully roofed queue, shaded regardless of sun position.
  • Partial shade — a mix of covered sections, tree canopy, and open switchbacks.
  • Mostly exposed — largely open sky with little structural shade.

The "right now" estimate combines that baseline with the sun's elevation angle for your selected time (higher sun = more overhead, harder-to-avoid exposure for open queues). It does not account for a specific building or tree casting a shadow on a specific spot at a specific minute — for that, use the compass and sun-path chart above alongside a park map to reason about which side of a wall or tree line will be shaded.

Queues get rebuilt, overflow into open areas at high capacity, and attractions occasionally close for refurbishment — always confirm current layout and operating status in the My Disney Experience app or with a Cast Member.
